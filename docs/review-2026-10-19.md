# Critical Review, 2026-10-19

A review of the project's motivation, its problem statement, its place in
the literature and its research process. It was written after the two-day
agent-driven oracle campaign of 2026-10-18/19, at the owner's request
("if there is no point in continuing, say so"). It is the reason for
[`charter.md`](charter.md), [`claims.md`](claims.md) and the rewritten
[`research_protocol.md`](research_protocol.md).

Sources:
- every document under `docs/`, now archived in [`archive/`](archive/) and
  [`experiments/archive/`](experiments/archive/);
- the oracle code and git history;
- an audit of the evidence trail;
- a literature check. Citations were verified against abstracts and
  proceedings pages, not the full papers, so check quantitative details
  before citing them.

The owner's goal: a system that takes camera images, is aware of the
mechanics, and drives a mechanical system (cartpole, later a robot arm).
Novelty is not required (A5). The secondary goals are deep understanding
and an agentic research method. All of it runs under a **two-week
time-box** with a decision at the end. This review judges the project
against those goals, not against "publish a paper".

## Part A — Motivation, question, literature

### A1. Stop the original project

The April question was whether a pixel world model with a learned
Lagrangian latent beats an RSSM at MPC control under shifted physics and
visual nuisance. Do not continue it.

- **It is crowded.**
  - Zhong & Leonard (NeurIPS 2020) learn Lagrangian dynamics from images
    on pendulum, cartpole and acrobot.
  - KeyCLD (Daems et al., Neurocomputing 2024) does keypoints plus
    constrained Lagrangian dynamics on dm_control cartpole, with explicit
    M, V and B.
  - DreamSAC (Tang et al., CVPR 2026) is a Hamiltonian world model from
    pixels, motivated by extrapolating to new physical properties.
  - HaM-World (2026, preprint) reports OOD-physics gains on dm_control.
  - PH-Dreamer (2026, preprint) uses port-Hamiltonian RSSM transitions.
- **Its core mechanism is ill-posed.** A model that has no input for the
  mass cannot predict a heavier pole zero-shot (H1-zs; the archived
  external review makes the same point). Generalization under parameter
  shift needs an adaptation or context mechanism, and at that point the
  question becomes system identification.
- **The benchmark cannot discriminate.** Pixel cartpole-swingup is
  saturated in distribution. Pole-mass shifts are dynamically invisible
  (A-OOD-2). Random-policy data cannot support swing-up MPC (A-DATA-1).
- **The model never worked as intended.** The end-to-end model put the
  pose into the nuisance `z`, not into `q` (A-ROUTE-1). The decoder that
  was meant to fix this could not learn the angle by gradient (A-FE-1).
- **The literature predicts the pixel result.** Botev et al. ("Which
  priors matter?", NeurIPS 2021 Datasets & Benchmarks) find that physics
  priors do not significantly beat standard models on pixels. The 3.8× →
  1.2–1.5× shrinkage measured here from true state to pixels is that
  finding again.

### A2. The current H1b result is real, and mostly true by construction

The result, reproduced from the raw JSONs by the new report tool (see
B-audit below): on true state, a Lagrangian whose cart-mass, gear and
potential terms are exposed as a 3–4 number context adapts from one
50-frame segment. Out of the trained range it is up to 6–7× better than a
context-conditioned MLP trained on the same five parameter values. Inside
the range the two are at parity, and a missing slot (gravity without a
potential slot) makes the Lagrangian 2–3× worse.

Why that is weaker than it looks:

1. **The slots were designed knowing the answer.** A diagonal-mass offset
   *is* how cart mass enters the equations. A log gain *is* a gear change.
   A potential scale *is* a gravity change. "A correctly specified
   grey-box model extrapolates in its parameters, a black-box context
   saturates, a misspecified one hurts" is textbook system identification.
   The ML literature has it piece by piece:
   - Wehenkel et al., *Robust Hybrid Learning With Expert Augmentation*
     (TMLR 2023), shows hybrid models fail out of the training parameter
     range unless the training data is augmented;
   - Han et al., *Adaptable Hamiltonian neural networks* (Phys. Rev.
     Research 2021), give an HNN the parameter as input, train it on
     about 4 values, and it predicts across an interval;
   - Takeishi & Kalousis, physics-integrated VAEs (NeurIPS 2021): grounded
     physics latents extrapolate only if regularized;
   - Lutter & Peters (IJRR 2023): physics-inspired networks win when their
     assumptions hold.

   No paper was found that tabulates the full triad (parity, growing
   advantage, misspecification penalty) in one sweep, so the result is a
   clean confirmation, not a discovery.
2. **The upper bracket is missing.** The oracle's `floor_true` uses the
   *true* parameters. Nobody fitted the *known cartpole equations* with
   unknown parameters (cart and pole mass, pole length, gear, gravity,
   damping) by least squares from the same k segments. That baseline is
   the natural upper bound. If it matches or beats `lag_ctx`, the learned
   Lagrangian buys only "you did not have to write the equations down",
   and on cartpole that is worth nothing.
3. **The structure-matched black box is missing.** The context MLP
   concatenates its context with the action input. A fair test of
   "structure" against "the parameter enters in the right place" needs a
   black box whose context enters where the parameter physically enters
   (for example, an MLP force with acceleration = (M₀ + ctx)⁻¹ f). It
   also needs a stronger generic context model: CoDA (Kirchmeyer et al.,
   ICML 2022), DyAd (Wang et al., NeurIPS 2022) or LEADS (Yin et al.,
   NeurIPS 2021).
4. **The adaptive-control literature already gives the standard
   answer.** "Identify a few parameters, then plan" is the default:
   - classical adaptive control;
   - UP-OSI (Yu et al., RSS 2017), which includes cart-pole swing-up;
   - Nagabandi et al. (ICLR 2019);
   - CaDM (Lee et al., ICML 2020);
   - RMA (Kumar et al., RSS 2021);
   - Neural-Fly (O'Connell et al., Science Robotics 2022).

   A contribution has to beat a known-model fit, or show that a *learned*
   structure is identifiable, or show a closed-loop gain.

### A3. What is non-obvious so far

These are worth keeping, but most are graded B or C in
[`claims.md`](claims.md):

- **Robustness flips with the noise distribution.** Under Gaussian pose
  noise the Lagrangian fits better than the MLP (0.25 px: nominal 0.018
  against 0.050). Under heavy-tailed or run-correlated real front-end
  errors it is about 2× *more* fragile. The proposed mechanism, unbounded
  q̇² Coriolis terms amplifying velocity spikes, is **untested**. The
  literature check found no work that combines heavy-tailed keypoint
  errors, structured dynamics and parameter adaptation. Robust
  errors-in-variables identification is the clearest open niche. Related
  tools:
  - Student-t filtering and smoothing (Roth et al. 2017);
  - Lightning Pose's ensemble Kalman smoothing (Biderman et al., Nature
    Methods 2024);
  - differentiable particle filters (Jonschkowski et al. 2018).
- **The start state, not the dynamics, limits accuracy under realistic
  noise.** Model-based shooting gives a 20× lower floor than finite
  differences.
- **Practical identifiability:**
  - an unconstrained actuation `B` destroys the physical meaning of `M`;
  - fixing the actuated coordinate restores it up to scale;
  - mass and gain form a near-gauge whose ratio is right within 3%.
- **Data and protocol pitfalls, now documented:**
  - the cart is out of view in 26% of frames;
  - rail contacts confound 30% of windows;
  - pole mass is dynamically invisible;
  - "length" scaled a rail;
  - dt was wrong by 2×;
  - the probe target was unbounded.
- **Rendering: the likelihood is right but gradient descent cannot find
  it.** Gradient descent through a correct renderer cannot find the pole
  angle (the loss is flat beyond 10°), while render-and-compare search
  finds it at 0.5°. This is consistent with known analysis-by-synthesis
  behaviour. Grade C: it rests on scratch scripts.

### A4. The question was never sharp, and the pivots were not decisions

"When do mechanical priors help visual control?" has no user, no decision
attached to it and no ceiling. Since October the project has moved from
OOD control to few-shot adaptation to the robustness of a pose front end.
Each move happened inside a decision rule's "otherwise, diagnose X next"
branch. Nobody decided to move.

### A5. The goal, clarified: do we need to invent anything?

The owner's goal (2026-10-19) is a system that takes camera images, is
aware of the mechanics, and drives a mechanical system: cartpole, later a
robot arm. Novelty is not required. The question is whether reaching the
goal needs anything original.

**For cartpole: no, by every available indication.** Two established
routes exist.

1. **End to end.** DreamerV3 and TD-MPC2 solve pixel cartpole-swingup in
   distribution, with returns of about 800–860.
   - They are not mechanics-aware.
   - They extrapolate poorly when the physics changes: cRSSM shows a
     context-free pixel Dreamer fails pole-length extrapolation.
   - They need far more compute than a laptop offers.
2. **Modular: classical adaptive control plus vision.** Calibrated
   camera → pose (a detector or even a colour/moment heuristic) → state
   estimator (EKF over the known model) → MPC or energy-shaping
   swing-up with an LQR catch → online least-squares identification of a
   few physical parameters.
   - This is mechanics-aware by construction.
   - It adapts by re-identifying parameters, which is exactly the H1b
     mechanism with the true equations.
   - Prior work: UP-OSI (Yu et al. 2017) does online identification for
     cart-pole swing-up from state; Jaques et al. (2020) estimate
     physical parameters from video with known equations and do
     vision-based MPC.

The project went the other way: it set out to *invent* the model (a
learned Lagrangian world model from pixels) before checking whether the
standard route already reaches the goal. The two days of oracles,
however, built most of what route 2 needs:
- the label-free front end (C-011);
- the camera-visibility finding (C-013);
- model-based start-state estimation (C-009);
- outlier rejection (C-010);
- MJCF-correct parameter shifts.

**Where invention could become necessary,** each case only after the
standard route is measured to fail there:
- the equations are unknown or substantially wrong (that is where a
  learned Lagrangian or a known-model-plus-residual hybrid earns its
  place: Q4);
- perception is too poor for a simple front end (clutter, occlusion,
  out-of-view): learned encoders or robust estimation (Q5);
- one model has to cover many systems.

**For a robot arm such as a UR5,** the arm's own dynamics are known
(URDF) and measured by joint encoders, so vision is not needed for the
arm's own mechanics. Vision matters for what the arm manipulates: a
swinging payload, a sliding or rolling object. The standard pipeline
generalizes to that case: object pose estimation → physics model of the
object → MPC. PIN-WM (Li et al., RSS 2025) is the learned-physics variant
of it. That would be a later node, once cartpole is closed.

### A6. Reformulation

**Q0.** Can we drive cartpole swing-up and balance from camera images
with a mechanics-aware pipeline built from standard components? Does it
keep working when the physical parameters change, and where exactly does
it fall short?

- **Tool:** the working camera-to-control pipeline, with online parameter
  identification.
- **Understanding:** each stage is measured against its own ceiling:
  - the true-state controller (E-001);
  - true poses plus matched noise (E-002);
  - true-state identification (E-003);
  - where useful, the Cramér–Rao bound.
- **Method:** the process redesign below, with this project as its
  testbed.

The program is in [`charter.md`](charter.md), and the records are under
[`experiments/`](experiments/):
- E-001: control ceiling on true state;
- E-002: camera in the loop;
- E-003: adaptation from vision;
- E-004 (conditional): the known-model bracket for H1b;
- E-005 (conditional): robust estimation.

None of them runs before the owner approves it.

## Part B — Process

### What works (keep)

- written predictions with probabilities;
- decision rules;
- at least three competing explanations for every surprise;
- the oracle ladder, intervention checks first;
- floors and ceilings.

These found the April errors in hours. The adversarial checks
(interpolation control, velocity split, dose-response) were the best
science in the project.

### What fails

The forensic checks in items 1–3 used `git show <commit>:<path>` against
each result's config. They are repeatable with the ledger.

1. **Pre-registration cannot be verified.** All 10 commits since the
   protocol was adopted add predictions *and* their results together.
   Git cannot show which came first.
2. **Most results came from uncommitted code.** Each result's recorded
   commit was compared with the code its config needed. **At least 87 of
   the 143 results** were produced by code that their recorded commit does
   not contain. Examples:
   - the whole oracle harness was first run at `3806fc1`, before
     `src/oracles/` was committed;
   - the simulated-shift runs (interpolation control, dose-response)
     record `803ac40`, which has no `--sim-shift`;
   - all noise and start-state runs (`309a231`);
   - the heuristic, rejection and heavy-tail runs (`cd1408e`);
   - the potential-slot runs (`1cd6669`);
   - the Huber runs (`d2b30cc`).

   Other gaps:
   - The code was committed later, but nothing proves it was the code
     that ran.
   - Results recorded no dirty flag, command line, data or versions.
   - Cache keys hashed only the config, so edited code could reuse stale
     cached cells.

   Only three headline batches pass this check, and only they are graded
   B in [`claims.md`](claims.md): the cart-mass matched baseline, gear,
   and gravity without a slot.
3. **Some evidence cannot be reproduced.** These claims rest on
   scratchpad scripts that were never committed:
   - the encoder bias and autocorrelation decomposition (`enc_diag.py`);
   - the flat render loss (`render_sens.py`);
   - render-and-compare at 0.54° (`render_search.py`);
   - the keypoint overfit (`overfit_kp.py`);
   - the A-ACT-1 mass-matrix comparison.

   On top of that, `runs/` is gitignored, there was no committed index of
   runs, and every cross-seed table was typed by hand.
4. **The registry turned into a lab notebook.** `hypotheses.md` reached
   1518 lines, about 1000 of them on H1b. The H1b body said `open` while
   the summary table said `supported`, and the summary "row" was a
   paragraph. Neither a human nor an agent could load "what we know"
   from it.
5. **Docs drift, and agents read the drift.**
   - `research_spec.md` was unchanged since April: old H1–H3, K1–K3, the
     "first systematic" claim.
   - The README was stale.
   - `CLAUDE.md`, loaded into every agent session, said the project was
     gated on the gate experiment, which had been stopped.
   - `current_status.md` was append-only at 634 lines.
6. **Diagnostic descent with no step-back.** The true-state result was
   followed by ten consecutive diagnostic steps in about 12 hours: noise →
   start state → Savitzky–Golay → encoders → debias → rejection → heavy
   tails → Huber → heuristic → run-length. Each step was locally
   rational. Nearly every decision rule ended in "otherwise, diagnose
   next"; none had a budget and none asked whether the node still served
   the root question. That is the circling.
7. **Effort was asymmetric (forking paths).** About six interventions
   were designed to help the Lagrangian:
   - `actuated_dims`;
   - `slot_scale`;
   - the potential slot;
   - model-based start;
   - rejection;
   - Huber.

   The baseline's design stayed fixed. Other researcher degrees of
   freedom:
   - thresholds (1.3) were round numbers, not effect sizes that matter;
   - a borderline prerequisite was accepted (0.308 against 0.3);
   - the aggregation ("mean over shifts", k1 vs k5) was chosen per test.
8. **The literature came after the building.** The novelty check for H1b
   happened after two days of H1b experiments, and it shows the claim is
   mostly known.
9. **Throughput outran review.** Twelve commits overnight with no human
   decision point, ending in a demo export before the claims had settled.

### B-audit: re-deriving the hand-typed tables

`scripts/research/report.py` re-aggregated the raw result files for the
headline H1b tables:
- the matched baseline;
- the interpolation control;
- the mass dose-response;
- gear;
- gravity with and without the potential slot.

**Every mean in the registry is reproduced to the reported precision.**
The "±" values in the registry are the *population* std over 3 seeds.
That understates the sample std by about 18%; for example, the
matched-baseline k1 ratio is 1.61 ± 0.148, not ± 0.12. The new report
tool prints the sample std.

The heavy side of the dose-response has sign-inconsistent seeds:
- ×3: 0.64 / 1.33 / 1.54;
- ×5 at k1: 1.58 / 1.21 / 0.77.

So "1.2 ± 0.4" there means "no effect", as the registry's own reading
said.

### Redesign (implemented 2026-10-19)

- **Evidence means committed code + a recorded run + a commit hash.**
  - `research.provenance.begin_run` refuses a dirty tree unless
    `--allow-dirty`, in which case the result is ungradeable.
  - Every result records its commit, dirty paths, argv, package versions,
    a data fingerprint and a source fingerprint.
  - Every run appends to the committed `results/ledger.jsonl`, with the
    sha256 of its result file.
  - Cache keys include the source fingerprint.
  - The 143 earlier results are imported as `legacy` entries.
- **Pre-registration is checkable.** A run of experiment E-NNN must start
  from a commit that already contains the record's *Prediction*, *Primary
  metric*, *Baselines* and *Decision rule*. Those sections must be
  unchanged since, and the record must be `approved`.
  `scripts/research/check.py` and `tests/test_research_hygiene.py`
  enforce this.
- **Generated tables:** `scripts/research/report.py E-NNN` writes the
  table into the record and `results/E-NNN/summary.json`.
- **A few small top-level docs.**
  - [`charter.md`](charter.md): the question tree, budget and stop date.
  - [`claims.md`](claims.md): graded facts with ledger evidence.
  - [`current_status.md`](current_status.md): at most one page.
  - [`hypotheses.md`](hypotheses.md): a compact index.

  The narrative lives in per-experiment records.
- **Rules against circling**, in
  [`research_protocol.md`](research_protocol.md):
  - a chain-depth limit (three "diagnose further" outcomes on one node
    force a zoom-out note and an owner decision);
  - every decision rule needs a stop branch and a justified threshold;
  - effort symmetry between arms;
  - bracketing by floor, black box, known model and bound;
  - a literature check before any novelty claim;
  - an adversarial review with a fixed prompt
    ([`review_prompt.md`](review_prompt.md)) at each decision point.

## Bottom line

- **Stop:** the pixel Lagrangian world model as the route to the goal.
  The code stays, frozen.
- **Do not write up yet:** H1b. It is a clean confirmation of a known
  effect, not needed for the goal, and unbracketed (E-004 if wanted).
- **Build the standard route first:** known-model MPC, a camera front end
  with an EKF, and online parameter identification (E-001 → E-002 →
  E-003). Measure each stage against its ceiling. Invent only where a
  stage fails against its ceiling.
- **Most likely outcome at the stop date:** cartpole from pixels works
  with standard components, and the honest answer to "do we need to
  invent something?" is "not for this system". The next decision is then
  whether to move to a system where the standard route is expected to
  struggle: unknown or partly wrong equations, a manipulated object
  observed through clutter.
